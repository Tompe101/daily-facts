## In-Depth Report
In a deeply candid and revealing interview published by The Guardian, Sonia Gandhi, the former president of the Indian National Congress, disclosed her profound and persistent fear concerning her husband, Rajiv Gandhi's, life. Speaking openly about the anxieties that shadowed her marriage into India's most influential political family, Mrs. Gandhi articulated a chilling premonition that ultimately materialized with tragic consequence.

### A Constant Shadow of Fear
Mrs. Gandhi recounted her harrowing internal struggle, stating, "My thought was always: he will be killed next." This stark admission underscores the immense personal burden and emotional toll endured by those at the epicentre of high-stakes Indian politics. Her words paint a vivid picture of a life lived under the constant threat of violence, a reality that deeply permeated her consciousness even before the fateful events of May 1991. The interview explores the psychological landscape of a woman who grappled with the severe dangers inherent in the political lineage she joined.

### Personal Sacrifice and Political Destiny
The comprehensive report by The Guardian delves into Mrs. Gandhi's initial reluctance to embrace political life, despite her prominent position within the Nehru-Gandhi dynasty. Her narrative highlights the profound personal sacrifices demanded by public service, especially within a family that has historically faced significant threats and losses. These revelations offer a rare glimpse into the private anguish that often accompanies public roles, providing a human dimension to a figure largely known for her political stoicism and leadership.

## Background & Context
The Nehru-Gandhi family has been a central force in Indian politics since the nation's independence, producing three Prime Ministers: Jawaharlal Nehru, Indira Gandhi, and Rajiv Gandhi. This lineage, however, has also been marked by profound tragedy. Indira Gandhi, Rajiv's mother and a sitting Prime Minister, was assassinated in 1984 by her own bodyguards in the aftermath of Operation Blue Star. This event propelled a reluctant Rajiv Gandhi into the Prime Minister's office, further entrenching the family's presence at the nation's helm.

Sonia Gandhi, an Italian-born woman, married Rajiv Gandhi in 1968. She initially maintained a private life, avoiding direct political involvement, a stance she held even after her husband became Prime Minister. Her reluctance was widely known, stemming from an acute awareness of the dangers faced by her in-laws. Rajiv Gandhi himself was assassinated on May 21, 1991, during an election campaign rally in Sriperumbudur, Tamil Nadu, by a suicide bomber from the Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE).
