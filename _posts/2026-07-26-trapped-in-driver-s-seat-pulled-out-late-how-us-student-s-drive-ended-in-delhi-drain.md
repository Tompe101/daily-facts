## Background & Context
Delhi, like many major metropolitan areas during the monsoon season, frequently experiences severe waterlogging and localized flooding. The city's infrastructure, including its drainage systems, has long been a subject of public debate and concern, particularly regarding its capacity to handle intense rainfall. Open drains and canals, often lacking adequate safety barriers or protective covers, pose significant hazards to commuters, especially during adverse weather conditions or at night.

Previous incidents involving vehicles or pedestrians falling into open drains during waterlogging events have been documented across various Indian cities, highlighting a recurring challenge in urban planning and infrastructure maintenance. These occurrences invariably bring into question the efficacy of municipal bodies in ensuring public safety on roadways, particularly concerning the identification and mitigation of high-risk zones. The current incident tragically illustrates the potential fatal consequences of such systemic vulnerabilities when combined with challenging environmental conditions.
