## Background & Context

The attack on King Fahd International Airport is not an isolated incident but rather fits within a broader pattern of cross-border assaults stemming from the protracted civil war in Yemen. Saudi Arabia has led a military coalition since 2015, intervening against the Houthi movement to restore the internationally recognized government. This intervention has been met with frequent retaliatory strikes by the Houthis, who possess an arsenal of drones and ballistic missiles.

Over the years, numerous Saudi cities, including the capital Riyadh and key economic hubs, have been targeted by Houthi projectiles. Airports like Abha International Airport and Najran Airport, situated closer to the Yemeni border, have been particularly vulnerable, experiencing direct hits and operational disruptions. These attacks highlight the Houthis' capability to strike deep into Saudi territory, posing a significant challenge to the Kingdom's air defense systems despite considerable investments.

The conflict in Yemen, often viewed as a proxy war between Saudi Arabia and Iran, fuels much of this cross-border aggression. Iran denies directly arming the Houthis but acknowledges political support. The geopolitical tensions in the Persian Gulf region, involving various state and non-state actors, contribute to an environment where such attacks are a regular, albeit devastating, occurrence, impacting regional stability and international shipping lanes.
