## Background & Context

### Indonesia's Position on the Ring of Fire
Indonesia is situated on the Pacific Ring of Fire, a horseshoe-shaped belt around the Pacific Ocean characterized by frequent seismic and volcanic activity. This geographical location makes the archipelago highly susceptible to earthquakes and tsunamis. The interaction of several major tectonic plates beneath the region — including the Pacific, Eurasian, and Indo-Australian plates — results in constant geological stress, leading to frequent tremors of varying magnitudes.

### History of Major Seismic Events
The nation has a long and tragic history of devastating earthquakes and tsunamis. Notable past events include the 2004 Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami, which killed hundreds of thousands across several countries, and numerous other significant quakes that have caused substantial loss of life and infrastructure damage throughout the 21st century. This recurrent geological instability necessitates robust early warning systems and resilient building codes, though their implementation varies across the vast and diverse archipelago.
