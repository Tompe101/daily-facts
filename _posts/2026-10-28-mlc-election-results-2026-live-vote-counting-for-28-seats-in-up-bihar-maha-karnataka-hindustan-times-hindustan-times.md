## Background & Context

The Legislative Councils, or Vidhan Parishads, serve as the upper chambers in the bicameral legislatures of six Indian states, including Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Maharashtra, and Karnataka. These councils play a vital role in legislative scrutiny, providing a platform for broader representation and deliberate discussion on state policies. Members of the Legislative Council (MLCs) are elected through a variety of mechanisms, including electoral colleges comprising members of local bodies, graduate constituencies, teacher constituencies, and election by Members of Legislative Assembly (MLAs), as well as nominations by the Governor.

The elections for these 28 seats in 2026 are part of the staggered retirement process for MLCs, ensuring continuity and regular renewal of the council's composition. Historically, these elections have often been less publicised than general assembly elections but are critical for the political parties in maintaining legislative strength. The results frequently reflect the prevailing political mood among specific professional groups or local bodies, offering a nuanced perspective on regional political currents.

Past elections for these councils have occasionally led to hung houses or shifts in control, impacting the ease with which state governments can pass legislation, particularly those requiring specific majorities in both houses. The current elections are therefore viewed as a continuation of these ongoing political contests, with parties aiming to either secure a comfortable majority or prevent the opposition from gaining undue influence in the legislative process.
