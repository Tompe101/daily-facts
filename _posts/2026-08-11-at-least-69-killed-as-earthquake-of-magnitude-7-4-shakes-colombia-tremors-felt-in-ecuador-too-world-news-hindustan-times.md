## Background & Context

The Andean region, encompassing Colombia and Ecuador, is situated within the "Ring of Fire," a horseshoe-shaped belt around the Pacific Ocean characterized by frequent seismic and volcanic activity. This geological setting is a result of the Nazca Plate continuously subducting beneath the South American Plate, a process that generates immense tectonic stress and, consequently, earthquakes. The complex interaction of these major plates, along with several smaller microplates, contributes to the region's high seismicity.

Historically, both Colombia and Ecuador have experienced numerous significant earthquakes, some causing substantial loss of life and extensive destruction. For instance, Ecuador was struck by a devastating 7.8 magnitude earthquake in 2016, which killed hundreds and caused widespread damage along its coast. Colombia, too, has a history of powerful quakes, underscoring the constant threat posed by its geological position. Governments in the region have implemented various measures, including seismic building codes and early warning systems, but the sheer power of events like the recent 7.4 magnitude tremor often overwhelms existing preparedness protocols.
