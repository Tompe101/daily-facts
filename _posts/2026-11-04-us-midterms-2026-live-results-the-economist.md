## Background & Context
### The Nature of US Midterm Elections
US Midterm Elections invariably occur halfway through a President's four-year term. Historically, these elections are often viewed as a referendum on the incumbent President and their party's performance. It is common for the President's party to lose seats in Congress during midterms, a phenomenon sometimes referred to as the "presidential penalty." This dynamic frequently results in a divided government, where the President's party controls the White House but faces an opposition-controlled Congress, leading to increased legislative gridlock or the necessity for bipartisan compromise.

### Historical Trends and Precursors
The political landscape leading up to the 2026 midterms will be shaped by the outcomes of the preceding presidential and congressional elections, as well as the prevailing economic and social conditions. Issues such as inflation, employment rates, healthcare, immigration, and geopolitical events typically dominate public discourse and influence voter sentiment. Previous midterm cycles have demonstrated the electorate's capacity for significant swings in power, responding to national mood and specific policy debates, thus setting a precedent for potential volatility in 2026.
