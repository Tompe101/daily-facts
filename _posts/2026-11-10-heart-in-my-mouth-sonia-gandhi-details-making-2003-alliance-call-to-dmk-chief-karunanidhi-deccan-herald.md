The 2003 alliance between the Congress and DMK was a significant precursor to the formation of the United Progressive Alliance (UPA), which would go on to govern India for a decade from 2004. For the Congress, securing the DMK's support was crucial for consolidating its position in Tamil Nadu and strengthening its national coalition ahead of the general elections. For the DMK, aligning with a national party like Congress offered mutual benefits in a complex political landscape. ## Background & Context

The early 2000s marked a period of significant political realignment in India. Following the Congress party's electoral setbacks in the late 1990s, forming broad-based alliances became an imperative for regaining national prominence. The DMK, a powerful regional party in Tamil Nadu, had historically maintained fluctuating relations with both the Congress and the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP). Prior to 2003, the DMK had been part of the BJP-led National Democratic Alliance (NDA), but political shifts and evolving state-level dynamics prompted a reconsideration of its alliances.

The Congress leadership, under Sonia Gandhi, was actively working towards forging a formidable coalition to counter the incumbent NDA government. The 2003 outreach to M. Karunanidhi was thus not an isolated event but a carefully considered strategic move within a broader effort to build the United Progressive Alliance. The delicate nature of this negotiation was amplified by previous instances of political rivalry and ideological differences between the two parties, making Mrs. Gandhi's call a moment of considerable political courage and foresight, aiming to bridge historical divides for a common electoral objective.
