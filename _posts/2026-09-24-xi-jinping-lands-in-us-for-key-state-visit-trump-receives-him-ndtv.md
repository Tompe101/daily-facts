## Background & Context
### A History of Complex Relations and Recent Tensions
The relationship between the United States and China has been characterized by a blend of cooperation and fierce competition across various domains for decades. While economic interdependency has grown substantially, geopolitical rivalries, ideological differences, and disputes over human rights, trade practices, and regional influence have frequently strained diplomatic ties. The period leading up to this visit has seen an escalation of rhetoric and policy actions, particularly concerning technology transfers, intellectual property, and strategic military posturing.

Former President Donald Trump's administration notably initiated a trade war with China, imposing significant tariffs and challenging Beijing on various fronts, including technology giant Huawei and the origins of the COVID-19 pandemic. His direct engagement style often led to unpredictable yet impactful interactions with President Xi Jinping. Following Mr. Trump's presidency, the subsequent administration has largely maintained a firm stance towards China, continuing to address perceived challenges while seeking areas for limited cooperation.

### The Unconventional Diplomatic Protocol
The reported reception of a visiting head of state by a former president deviates significantly from established international diplomatic protocol, which typically mandates reception by the sitting head of state or designated high-ranking officials of the current administration. While informal meetings between former leaders and foreign dignitaries are not unheard of, a formal reception upon arrival for a state visit is unprecedented. This extraordinary development raises immediate questions about coordination between the current and former administrations, and the message it intends to convey both domestically and internationally.
