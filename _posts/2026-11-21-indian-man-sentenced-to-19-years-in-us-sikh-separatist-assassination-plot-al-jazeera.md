## In-Depth Report
### Indian National Sentenced in US Assassination Plot
Nikhil Gupta, a 53-year-old Indian national, has been sentenced to 19 years in federal prison by a U.S. court for his involvement in a foiled plot to assassinate Gurpatwant Singh Pannun, a prominent Sikh separatist and U.S. citizen. The sentencing by U.S. District Judge Victor Marrero in Manhattan marks a critical development in a case that has strained diplomatic relations between Washington and New Delhi. Gupta was convicted of murder-for-hire, a charge stemming from his alleged role in orchestrating the plot from India.

Gupta's conviction and subsequent sentencing follow his extradition from the Czech Republic to the United States last year. Prosecutors presented evidence detailing how Gupta was directed by an unnamed Indian government official to hire a hitman to kill Pannun. Gupta was allegedly in contact with a confidential informant, who was in fact an undercover U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) agent, believing the informant was a professional assassin. This interaction formed the core of the prosecution's case.

The U.S. Justice Department underscored the gravity of the offense, stating that the sentencing sends a clear message that such extraterritorial assassination attempts on U.S. soil will not be tolerated. U.S. Attorney Damian Williams emphasized the unwavering commitment to protecting the lives of American citizens and upholding the rule of law, regardless of where such plots originate. The extensive investigation involved multiple U.S. agencies, highlighting the significant resources deployed to uncover and neutralize the threat.

This verdict is anticipated to reverberate through international legal and diplomatic channels. The court's decision concludes a significant chapter in the legal proceedings against Gupta, yet it opens broader discussions regarding national sovereignty, the protection of dissidents, and the conduct of state actors on foreign soil. The U.S. government maintains its vigilance against any attempts to compromise national security or endanger its citizens.

## Background & Context
The plot came to light when U.S. federal prosecutors unsealed an indictment in November 2023, alleging that an Indian government official had directed Gupta to arrange Pannun’s assassination. Gurpatwant Singh Pannun is a New York-based lawyer and a leading figure in the "Sikhs for Justice" movement, which advocates for an independent Sikh state called Khalistan. He has been designated a terrorist by India, which views his activities as a threat to its national security and territorial integrity.

The indictment detailed how Gupta allegedly communicated with the Indian government official, referred to as "CC-1," to facilitate the murder. The plot reportedly involved a payment of $100,000 to the assassin. This revelation prompted strong condemnation from the U.S. government and led to significant diplomatic pressure on India. In response, India's Ministry of External Affairs acknowledged the U.S. concerns and stated it had constituted a high-level inquiry committee to investigate the allegations, affirming its commitment to rule-based international order.

The case emerged amidst broader concerns raised by Canada regarding alleged Indian government involvement in the assassination of Hardeep Singh Nijjar, another Sikh separatist leader, in Surrey, British Columbia, in June 2023. While the two cases are distinct, they collectively point to a pattern of alleged extraterritorial actions by Indian intelligence agencies against Sikh separatists residing in Western countries, intensifying scrutiny on India's foreign policy and intelligence operations.
