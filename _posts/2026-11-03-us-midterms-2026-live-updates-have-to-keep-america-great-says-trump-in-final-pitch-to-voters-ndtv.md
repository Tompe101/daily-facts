## Background & Context

The US Midterm Elections, held every four years, serve as a critical democratic process where voters elect new members of Congress and state-level officials, typically halfway through a president's term. These elections often reflect public satisfaction or dissatisfaction with the incumbent administration and can significantly alter the legislative agenda and political power balance in Washington D.C. Control of the House of Representatives and the Senate is frequently contested, impacting the ease with which a sitting president can enact their policy objectives.

Donald Trump, who served as the 45th President of the United States from 2017 to 2021, has maintained a robust public presence and a strong influence over the Republican Party since leaving office. His political movement, centered around the "Make America Great Again" and "Keep America Great" mottos, has continued to inspire a devoted base of supporters. His endorsements carry considerable weight in Republican primary and general elections, often determining the viability of candidates.

Leading up to the 2026 Midterms, various national and local issues have dominated public discourse, including economic inflation, immigration policies, social issues, and geopolitical events. These factors create a complex electoral landscape, where candidates strive to address voter concerns while aligning with their party's platforms. Trump's engagement in this cycle is consistent with his strategy of remaining a central figure in American politics, leveraging his rallies and media appearances to shape public opinion and mobilize his base.
