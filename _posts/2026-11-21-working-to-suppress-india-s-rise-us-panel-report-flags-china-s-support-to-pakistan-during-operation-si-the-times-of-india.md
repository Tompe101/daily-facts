## Background & Context

### Operation Siachen: A Critical Engagement
Operation Siachen refers to the military conflict between India and Pakistan over the control of the Siachen Glacier region, located in the eastern Karakoram range in the Himalayas. The conflict officially began in 1984 with India's Operation Meghdoot, which pre-emptively seized control of the glacier, establishing a strategic advantage. This high-altitude, inhospitable terrain has been a point of contention due to its strategic importance, providing vantage points over critical supply routes and territorial claims. The initial phase of the conflict was marked by intense skirmishes and the establishment of permanent military posts in extreme conditions.

### The China-Pakistan Strategic Alliance
The strategic relationship between China and Pakistan dates back decades, characterized by extensive military, economic, and diplomatic cooperation. This alliance is often perceived as a counterweight to India's regional power. China has been a primary supplier of military hardware to Pakistan, including advanced weaponry and technology. Furthermore, the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC), a flagship project of China's Belt and Road Initiative, deeply intertwines the economies and strategic interests of both nations, solidifying their bilateral ties.

### Historical Geopolitical Rivalries
The broader context involves a history of geopolitical rivalry between India and China, marked by border disputes, differing strategic interests, and competition for influence in Asia. Both nations are rising global powers, and their interactions often reflect a complex mix of cooperation and competition. Similarly, India and Pakistan have a long history of conflict, rooted in territorial disputes and ideological differences, making the South Asian region inherently susceptible to external influences and strategic interventions by major powers.
