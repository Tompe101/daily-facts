## Background & Context
The Himalayan belt, where Uttarakhand is situated, is one of the most seismically active regions globally. This high seismicity is primarily attributed to the ongoing collision between the Indian and Eurasian tectonic plates. The Indian plate is continuously pushing northward into the Eurasian plate, leading to immense geological stress accumulation along various fault lines beneath the mountain range. This stress is periodically released in the form of earthquakes.

Uttarakhand has historically experienced several significant seismic events, with devastating consequences. Notable past earthquakes include the 1991 Uttarkashi earthquake and the 1999 Chamoli earthquake, both of which caused widespread destruction and loss of life. These historical events serve as a stark reminder of the region's inherent seismic risk and the necessity for robust construction standards and effective disaster preparedness mechanisms.

The frequent tremors felt in the Delhi-NCR region, often originating from the Himalayan faults, are a direct consequence of its geological proximity to this active collision zone. While Delhi itself has a complex geology with several local fault lines, many of the stronger tremors felt in the capital are distant effects of larger seismic events occurring in the Himalayas or surrounding areas.
