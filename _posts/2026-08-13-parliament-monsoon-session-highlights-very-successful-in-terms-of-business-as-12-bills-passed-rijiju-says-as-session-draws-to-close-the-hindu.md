## Background & Context
The Monsoon Session is one of the three annual parliamentary sessions held in India, typically commencing in July and concluding in August. It serves as a crucial period for legislative business, allowing the government to introduce and pass Bills, engage in debates on matters of national importance, and hold the executive accountable through various parliamentary mechanisms. Historically, these sessions have been pivotal in shaping India's legal and policy landscape.

Prior to the commencement of this session, there were significant expectations regarding the government's legislative priorities, particularly concerning economic reforms and social legislation. The political climate often dictates the tenor of these sessions, with the ruling party aiming to pass its agenda efficiently, while the opposition seeks to scrutinize legislation and raise public concerns. The effective functioning of the session is often measured by the number of Bills passed and the quality of debates held.

The government had outlined a comprehensive legislative agenda for the Monsoon Session, including several Bills that had been pending from previous sessions or were newly introduced to address contemporary challenges. The commitment to parliamentary productivity is a recurring theme for the current administration, which consistently seeks to demonstrate efficiency in lawmaking.
