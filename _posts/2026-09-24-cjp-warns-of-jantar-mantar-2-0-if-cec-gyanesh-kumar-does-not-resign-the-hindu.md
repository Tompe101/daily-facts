## TL;DR Summary
*   The Campaign for Judicial Accountability and Reforms (CJP) has issued a stern warning, demanding the resignation of Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar.
*   CJP threatens a nationwide protest, dubbed 'Jantar Mantar 2.0', if Kumar does not step down, citing concerns over the integrity of his appointment.
*   The demand stems from allegations surrounding the selection process of Election Commissioners, which excluded the Chief Justice of India.

## In-Depth Report

### CJP Issues Ultimatum to CEC Gyanesh Kumar
The Campaign for Judicial Accountability and Reforms (CJP) has delivered a significant ultimatum, calling for the immediate resignation of Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar. In a recent statement that has garnered widespread attention, the civil society group explicitly warned of a potential 'Jantar Mantar 2.0' movement should its demand not be met. This direct challenge underscores growing concerns regarding the impartiality and transparency of the Election Commission of India (ECI).

### Allegations of Procedural Irregularities
The core of CJP's demand revolves around the controversial appointment process that led to Mr. Kumar's selection. The organisation asserts that the procedure circumvented established norms designed to ensure the ECI's independence. Specifically, CJP highlights the recent legislative changes that altered the composition of the selection committee, notably excluding the Chief Justice of India from the panel responsible for appointing Election Commissioners. This exclusion, CJP argues, has compromised the neutrality of the selection mechanism and casts a shadow over the legitimacy of the appointments.

### The Specter of 'Jantar Mantar 2.0'
The reference to 'Jantar Mantar 2.0' is a potent symbol, invoking memories of large-scale public protests that have historically originated from Jantar Mantar in New Delhi, such as the anti-corruption movement. By using this analogy, CJP signals its intent to mobilise public opinion and potentially initiate a widespread civil disobedience campaign if its demands for Mr. Kumar's resignation are not addressed. This suggests a deepening confrontation between civil society and the government over institutional integrity.

### Broader Context of Electoral Integrity
This development occurs amidst a broader national debate concerning the autonomy of India's constitutional bodies. The Supreme Court has previously underscored the imperative of maintaining the ECI's independence to safeguard democratic principles. CJP's current stand reflects a growing apprehension that recent governmental actions may be eroding the foundational pillars of electoral fairness and public trust in democratic processes. The situation is thus evolving into a critical test for both the executive and independent oversight mechanisms.

## Background & Context

The controversy surrounding the appointment of Election Commissioners gained prominence with the enactment of the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023. This legislation replaced a Supreme Court directive that mandated a selection committee comprising the Prime Minister, the Leader of the Opposition, and the Chief Justice of India. Under the new law, the Chief Justice was replaced by a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the Prime Minister.

This legislative change sparked immediate criticism from opposition parties and civil society organisations, who argued that it significantly diluted the independence of the ECI by giving the executive a predominant role in the appointment process. Critics contended that such a composition could potentially lead to politically motivated appointments, thereby undermining the impartial functioning of the election body, which is crucial for upholding free and fair elections.

The appointments of Gyanesh Kumar and Sukhbir Singh Sandhu as Election Commissioners in March 2024, following the resignation of Arun Goel and the retirement of Anup Chandra Pandey, occurred under the provisions of this new Act. These appointments drew particular scrutiny due to the new selection mechanism and the speed with which they were made, further intensifying the debate over executive influence on the ECI and prompting legal challenges to the new appointment law itself in the Supreme Court.

## Why It Matters (Impact Analysis)

The CJP's warning and the demand for the resignation of Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar carry profound implications for the credibility and independence of the Election Commission of India. A weakened or perceived as compromised ECI can erode public trust in electoral outcomes, which is foundational to the democratic process. This can lead to widespread skepticism regarding the fairness of future elections, potentially destabilising the political landscape.

Should the CJP's threat of 'Jantar Mantar 2.0' materialise, it could trigger significant civil unrest and large-scale protests across the nation. Such a movement would not only disrupt public life but also put immense pressure on the government, potentially forcing it to reconsider its stance on institutional independence. The social and political ramifications of such a widespread protest could be substantial, affecting governance and public order.

Moreover, this development highlights a critical challenge to India's system of checks and balances. The ongoing debate over the ECI's appointment process underscores the tension between executive power and the autonomy of independent institutions. The outcome of this standoff could set a precedent for the future of other constitutional bodies, influencing their ability to function without undue executive interference and thereby impacting the overall health and resilience of India's democratic framework.
