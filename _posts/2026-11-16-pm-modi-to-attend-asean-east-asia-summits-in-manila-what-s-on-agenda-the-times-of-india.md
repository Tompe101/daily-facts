## In-Depth Report

### Prime Minister Modi Confirms Attendance at Crucial Regional Summits

Prime Minister Narendra Modi is slated to attend the pivotal Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) and East Asia Summits, scheduled to take place in Manila, Philippines. This forthcoming participation marks a significant diplomatic engagement, reinforcing India's commitment to regional stability, economic integration, and multilateral cooperation. The summits, drawing leaders from across the Indo-Pacific, serve as crucial platforms for deliberating on a diverse array of geopolitical and socio-economic matters pertinent to the region.

### Key Agendas and Strategic Imperatives

The agenda for both summits is expected to be comprehensive, focusing on several critical pillars. Discussions are anticipated to revolve around enhancing regional connectivity, fostering sustainable economic growth, and addressing common security threats such as terrorism, maritime piracy, and transnational crime. Furthermore, leaders will likely deliberate on strategies for a free, open, and inclusive Indo-Pacific, emphasizing adherence to international law and norms. India is poised to advocate for deeper economic linkages, particularly through its existing free trade agreements and proposed connectivity initiatives.

### India's 'Act East Policy' in Focus

Prime Minister Modi's presence at these summits underscores the continued vitality of India's 'Act East Policy', which seeks to deepen engagement with Southeast Asian nations. This policy emphasizes comprehensive cooperation across trade, investment, defence, and cultural exchanges. India views ASEAN as a central pillar of its Indo-Pacific vision and a crucial partner in maintaining regional balance and prosperity. The summits provide an invaluable opportunity to review progress on existing partnerships and forge new avenues for collaboration.

### Anticipated Bilateral Engagements and Outcomes

Beyond the multilateral discussions, the summits are expected to facilitate numerous bilateral meetings between Prime Minister Modi and other participating heads of state and government. ## Background & Context

The Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) was established in 1967 and has evolved into a formidable regional bloc comprising ten member states. It plays a central role in fostering economic growth, social progress, and cultural development within Southeast Asia, while also promoting regional peace and stability. India became a sectoral dialogue partner of ASEAN in 1992, subsequently upgrading to a full dialogue partner in 1995, and then to a strategic partner in 2012, reflecting a progressive deepening of ties.

The East Asia Summit (EAS), formed in 2005, is a premier leaders-led forum in the Indo-Pacific region, bringing together 18 member countries. It provides a platform for strategic dialogue and cooperation on key political, security, and economic challenges. Its membership includes the ten ASEAN member states, along with Australia, China, India, Japan, New Zealand, Republic of Korea, Russia, and the United States. India has been an active participant, leveraging the EAS to advance its vision for a rules-based regional order.

India's 'Look East Policy', initiated in the early 1990s, aimed at cultivating extensive economic and strategic relations with Southeast Asian nations to enhance its standing as a regional power and counter China's growing influence. This policy was subsequently upgraded to the 'Act East Policy' in 2014, signaling a more proactive and action-oriented engagement, moving beyond just economic ties to encompass broader strategic and defence cooperation.

## Why It Matters (Impact Analysis)

Prime Minister Modi's participation in these summits holds significant geopolitical weight, underscoring India's commitment to being a net security provider and a reliable partner in the Indo-Pacific. In an increasingly complex global landscape marked by evolving power dynamics, India's active role helps reinforce a multipolar regional order, contributing to the balance of power and fostering stability against unilateral assertions. This engagement is vital for projecting India's strategic autonomy and its vision for inclusive regional architecture.

Economically, India's presence can catalyze further integration with the vibrant Southeast Asian markets. Discussions on trade facilitation, investment promotion, and infrastructure development, such as the India-Myanmar-Thailand Trilateral Highway, have the potential to unlock new avenues for economic growth and job creation across the participating nations. Enhanced connectivity and supply chain resilience discussed at these forums are crucial for India's economic prosperity and its aspiration to become a global manufacturing hub.

Furthermore, these summits are crucial for addressing shared security challenges. Collaborative approaches to counter-terrorism, maritime domain awareness, and humanitarian assistance/disaster relief (HADR) operations are essential for regional stability. India's contributions in these areas, coupled with its emphasis on a free and open Indo-Pacific, are instrumental in safeguarding common interests, ensuring freedom of navigation, and upholding a rules-based international order vital for global trade and security.
